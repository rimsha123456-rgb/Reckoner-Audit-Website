Academy Trust Bursary Schemes: Can You Use Charitable Funds?

Muhammad Zeeshan 11 Jun, 2026 6 min read

Academy trusts increasingly use bursary schemes to support pupils with costs including books, equipment, tuition, trips, and wellbeing support. A central question emerges: can these costs be paid from trust funds, and is prior permission necessary?

The short answer is usually yes — but the detail matters.

Using Unrestricted Funds

Unrestricted funds, including general annual grants, may typically fund bursary schemes when they:

  • Fall within the trust's charitable objects (typically the advancement of education)
  • Demonstrably benefit pupils, directly or indirectly
  • Remain reasonable and proportionate in value and eligibility
  • Deliver genuine educational benefit rather than personal benefit

Three underlying tests apply: the public benefit test requires supporting educational access and participation; equity and fairness demand objectively justified eligibility criteria; and value for money requires the governing board to be satisfied about appropriate use of public funds. Hardship funds, uniform grants, and curriculum access support comfortably fit this category.

Using Restricted Funds

Restricted funds can be used only where the original restriction permits it. Before allocating restricted money, trustees must examine donation agreements and grant conditions carefully. Funds designated for pupil support, hardship, widening participation, enrichment, access, or wellbeing typically support bursaries. However, funds tied to specific capital projects or named purposes cannot be redirected without breaching trust law. For ambiguous wording, trustees should document their reasoning, maintain clear accounting separation, and seek donor consent or legal advice if doubt persists.

Prior Approval Requirements

Most schemes require no advance approval when they are clearly educational, modest in scale, consistent with sector practice, and properly governed. However, schemes considered "novel, contentious or repercussive" may attract scrutiny. Schemes drawing concern typically involve large or unconditional payments, appear as enrolment incentives, risk public criticism, set sectoral precedents, or benefit narrow groups without clear justification. Established hardship arrangements with controlled, conditional payments tied to educational participation face fewer challenges.

Targeting Specific Year Groups

Limiting schemes to particular year groups — such as key intake years or years with attainment gaps — is permissible. Restrictions must be rational, non-arbitrary, and genuinely educational, avoiding unjustified exclusions. Trustees should monitor for indirect discrimination, document rationale and evidence, establish periodic review, and obtain board approval.

Framing Considerations

A critical distinction exists between schemes supporting pupils based on educational need versus those rewarding new enrolment. Supporting educational need constitutes legitimate assistance; rewarding enrolment can appear as an improper financial inducement, making schemes contentious and potentially problematic regarding public fund use. The safer design anchors eligibility on need, disadvantage, or documented educational rationale rather than enrolment status alone.

Summary

Defensible bursary schemes are:

  • Reimbursement-based rather than cash payments
  • Supported by written eligibility policies and complete audit trails
  • Justified by educational need rather than enrolment
  • Approved by the board and reviewed periodically

Establishing these foundations ensures alignment with both charity law and academy sector expectations.
