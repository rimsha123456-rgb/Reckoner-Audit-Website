When each threshold applies

How the Making Tax Digital for Income Tax check works

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is being introduced in phases from 6 April 2026. Once mandated, a sole trader or landlord must keep digital records, send HMRC a quarterly update for each business, and submit a final declaration in place of the old Self Assessment return, all using compatible software.

What counts as qualifying income

The threshold is tested against combined gross income (before expenses) from self employment and property only. Income from employment taxed under PAYE, pensions, savings interest, dividends and partnership profit shares does not count. Where income comes from both a qualifying source and a non qualifying source, only the qualifying part is measured.

Which year HMRC looks at

HMRC checks the qualifying income reported on the Self Assessment return due on 31 January before each start date. The 2024/25 return sets the April 2026 position, the 2025/26 return sets April 2027, and the 2026/27 return sets April 2028. If a business traded for less than a full year, the figure is annualised. HMRC writes to confirm when a taxpayer must join.

Staying in and exemptions

Once in the regime, a taxpayer stays in until qualifying income has been below the relevant threshold for three consecutive years, or the qualifying income sources cease. Partnerships and LLPs are outside the current phases and cannot yet sign up. Some taxpayers are exempt, for example those who are digitally excluded. Taxpayers below the thresholds can still choose to sign up voluntarily.

This is an indicative guide. Confirm the position using HMRC's own tool, Check if and when you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, and take professional advice on borderline or complex cases.

Frequently asked questions

Take home pay is your gross salary less Income Tax, National Insurance, any pension contribution and any student loan repayment. Income Tax uses your personal allowance and the tax bands for where you live, National Insurance is charged on earnings above the primary threshold, and student loan is a percentage of earnings above your plan threshold.
The tools use the UK rates and thresholds announced for the 2024/25, 2025/26 and 2026/27 tax years, cross checked against HMRC, gov.scot and the Student Loans Company. The Income Tax bands, personal allowance and National Insurance thresholds are frozen to 2030/31. Figures should still be reviewed each tax year and after each Budget.
Sole traders and landlords are brought in over three phases: from 6 April 2026 for qualifying income over £50,000, from 6 April 2027 for over £30,000, and from 6 April 2028 for over £20,000. Partnerships and LLPs are outside the current phases. Use the checker above to see the position for a given client.
Qualifying income is the combined gross income, before expenses, from self employment and property. It does not include employment income taxed under PAYE, pensions, savings interest, dividends or a partnership profit share.
After the £500 dividend allowance, dividends are taxed at 10.75% in the basic rate band, 35.75% in the higher rate band and 39.35% in the additional rate band for 2026/27. The basic and higher rates each rose by 2 percentage points from 6 April 2026. Dividend rates are the same across the UK, including Scotland.
A sole trader pays Income Tax on profit above the personal allowance at the rates for where they live, plus Class 4 National Insurance at 6% on profit between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above that. Class 2 National Insurance is now voluntary for most. The sole trader tab works this out.
